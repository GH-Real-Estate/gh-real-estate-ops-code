1. What this package is - and is not
This four-volume package is the most defensible form of a 'complete compilation' for the facts currently known: a dated applicability
map, operative rules, transaction controls, and a comprehensive official-source register. It does not copy every page of the Internal
Revenue Code, Kansas Statutes, municipal code, IRS publications, or the copyrighted FASB Codification. A raw legal corpus would be
harder for a project to retrieve correctly, would mix active and irrelevant rules, and would become stale without warning.
Binding-output warning
ChatGPT cannot make an accounting answer legally binding. Only the governing law, valid contracts, filed elections/returns, and decisions
of the responsible licensed professionals or authorities can do that. The project should produce decision support and draft bookkeeping
entries, not legal opinions or return-signing assurance.

The three detailed volumes divide the source set by authority: federal tax and GAAP; Kansas law and tax; and Overland Park /
Johnson County administration. Every material rule is tagged with an official source ID and full URL. If a live source has changed after
the checked-through date, the live source controls.

4. Mandatory ChatGPT answer protocol
 1. Start with the transaction date or tax year, entity, unit/property, payment method, and whether the question asks for GAAP books,
    tax books, a tax return position, or legal compliance.

 2. Give four separate conclusions when relevant: clean financial-statement treatment; federal income-tax treatment; Kansas
    treatment; Overland Park / Johnson County treatment.

 3. Cite the sourcebook ID and identify the legal weight. Never call an IRS publication, FAQ, FASB update, or internal policy a statute.

 4. When GAAP and tax differ, post or propose a book-tax adjustment. Do not distort the accounting ledger to force it to equal the tax
    return.

 5. For any fee, deposit, refund, or chargeback, use the executed lease for that tenant. Do not infer authorization from Zoho item
    names or the target fee policy.

 6. For repairs, renovations, appliances, permits, and closing costs, ask for the invoice, scope, unit of property, placed-in-service date,
    and related work before classifying.

 7. For vendor reporting, ask for W-9, entity type, amount paid during the calendar year, payment method, and whether a card or
    third-party network settled the payment.

 8. Never invent a threshold, form edition, rate, deadline, election, account code, or lease term. Mark unresolved points 'verify' and
    identify the exact document or professional needed.

 9. Treat tenant SSNs, bank data, and payment credentials as prohibited project-source data. Store only redacted evidence and the
    minimum facts needed for the entry.

 10. End material recommendations with a verification block: sources used, assumptions, missing evidence, proposed entry, tax
    adjustment, and escalation trigger.

6. Immediate project controls and cleanup priorities
 1. Confirm that the Overland Park rental license for the fourplex is active, tied to the correct owner/address, and calendared for
    biennial renewal.

 2. Verify federal tax classification and accounting method from the latest return and entity-election records before making Schedule E
    or disregarded-entity assumptions final.

 3. Build a permanent fixed-asset and depreciation schedule: land, building, unit-level conversion dates, 2026 renovations, appliances,
    land improvements, accumulated depreciation, and disposition history.

 4. Reclassify security deposits to tenant-level liabilities and create a move-out checklist that meets Kansas itemization and timing
    rules.

 5. Obtain attorney review of the holding-deposit form and reconcile every signed lease to the fee policy before automating charges.

 6. Adopt a prospective written capitalization / de minimis accounting policy before the first day of the tax year in which it will be used;
    retain annual election statements with the return.

 7. Require W-9 collection before non-card vendor payment, preserve payment method, and run a calendar-year 1099 review using
    the 2026 threshold and exceptions.

 8. Confirm whether Johnson County ever received an initial personal-property rendition for reportable assets; after that, file by March
    15 when a required change occurs.

 9. Separate federal, Kansas, and book-basis adjustments in year-end workpapers, including advance rent, depreciation, passive
    losses, and owner-occupied conversion allocations.

 10. Run the annual and event-driven refresh checklist in Section 10 before using the package for a new tax year or changed
    operation.

10. Refresh and change-control checklist
Run annually each January, again before filing, and immediately when a trigger occurs.

 1. Replace tax-year forms and instructions; verify mileage, depreciation, QBI, estimated-tax, and information-return thresholds.

 2. Check new federal public laws, Treasury Decisions, Internal Revenue Bulletins, IRS disaster relief, and final regulations.

 3. Check Kansas session-law changes, KDOR notices/forms, Secretary of State fees/dates, personal-property guidance, and
    unclaimed-property instructions.

 4. Check Overland Park Municipal Code Chapter 5.75, master fee schedule, rental inspection guidance, sales/transient guest tax
    rates, and permit fee schedule.

 5. Check Johnson County value notices, appeal deadline, property-tax calendar, and personal-property rendition status.

 6. Refresh the signed-lease register, loan covenants, insurance requirements, occupancy/use, and owner/member status.

 7. Trigger immediate re-scope for: short-term stays; employees; new member; corporate election; sale/refinance; casualty; second
    property; major rehabilitation; substantial tenant services; lawsuit; lender GAAP covenant.

 8. Archive the superseded PDF package with its checked-through date. Never silently overwrite the historical package used for a
    prior-year return decision.
