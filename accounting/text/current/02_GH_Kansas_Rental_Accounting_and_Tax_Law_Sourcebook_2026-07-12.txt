1. Scope and applicability
This volume is tailored to a Kansas domestic LLC owning a long-term residential fourplex in Overland Park. It includes rules that
directly affect accounting records, tenant liabilities, taxes, entity records, and filing. Employment, short-term lodging, abandoned
belongings, nonresident-service withholding, and CPA-practice rules are conditional.
Lease and fee warning
The supplied target fee policy may not match existing signed leases. Kansas does not make an internal policy enforceable. Before
invoicing or automating a fee, use the applicable executed lease and obtain Kansas counsel review for unconscionability or disputed
terms.

K.S.A. 58-2550 does not itself state that deposits must be held in a separate escrow account or earn interest. A dedicated bank
account can still be a strong internal control, and a lease or other obligation could impose additional requirements. Do not state that
segregation is a Kansas statutory requirement without another applicable source.


3. Unclaimed tenant refunds and credit balances
 - A stale or uncashed refund check remains a liability. Do not reverse it to rent income merely because a check expired or mail was
   returned.

 - Kansas property definitions expressly include security deposits, refunds, credit balances, overpayments, and checks. The five-year
   all-other-property period is the likely rule for an uncashed tenant refund, but classify each item before calendaring.

 - Holder reports are generally due before November 1 for the 12 months preceding July 1. Due diligence for items of at least $100
   with a usable address generally occurs not more than 120 and not less than 60 days before filing.

 - The limited no-report exception applies when no individual item exceeds $100 and aggregate property is under $250, unless the
   Treasurer requires a report. Do not treat that exception as permission to recognize income.

 - Keep owner/property records at least 10 years after the property becomes reportable and retain report/remittance confirmations.

6. Kansas LLC books, contributions, and distributions
 - Maintain organization documents, member/manager and contribution records, financial-condition information, and tax returns.
   Electronic records must be convertible to readable paper within a reasonable time.

 - Book owner cash/property contributions to member equity, not other income. Preserve amount/date/property value and
   operating-agreement effect.

 - Book owner draws to distributions/equity, not expense. Apply statutory solvency limits and the operating agreement before
   distribution.

 - File the biennial information report by April 15 in years matching the parity of the formation year. Use the live Secretary of State
   portal for current fee and status.


7. Vendor and state information reporting
When a federal information return is required for a Kansas resident or Kansas-taxable payee, Kansas copy filing may be required.
Current KDOR specifications use a January 31 due date and electronic filing for more than 50 forms. Preserve W-9, payment method,
federal form decision, Kansas nexus, and acceptance confirmation. Federal payment-card reporting rules can prevent duplicate payer
Forms 1099, but Kansas filing follows the federal form actually required.

9. Kansas record-retention controls
KDOR's general baseline is current year plus at least three prior years, and sales/use-tax records are commonly open for at least three
years. That is not sufficient for every GH record. Keep tax returns/elections and entity records permanently; asset basis/depreciation
until after disposition limitation periods; unclaimed-property records at least 10 years after reportable; and lease/deposit evidence
through all refund, dispute, and unclaimed-property periods.
